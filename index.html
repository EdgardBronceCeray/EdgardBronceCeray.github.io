About the Author

Edgard Bronce-Ceray writes plays, novels, and translations that reimagine the monuments of French literature for contemporary readers. His work brings verse drama and swashbuckling narrative back to the English-speaking page — and, increasingly, to the stage.

The Rostand Project is his theatrical flagship — Edmond Rostand translated, extended, and transposed. Its first cycle, The Panache Trilogy, frames the first English translation of Cyrano de Bergerac to preserve the alexandrine metre — the twelve-syllable line that gives French classical verse its music — with an original prequel and sequel. The Project now turns to Chantecler: a new translation is forthcoming, and its modern transposition, The Teacher's Song, sets Rostand's fable of the rooster who sings up the sun in a present-day classroom, facing the emergence of artificial intelligence.

He is also the creator of The Zévaco Project — the first attempt ever made, by any publisher in over a century, to translate the complete novels of Michel Zévaco into English. Twenty-seven volumes have appeared to date: the complete ten-volume Les Pardaillan, Le Capitan in two volumes, and fifteen standalone novels and diptychs, from Borgia! and Nostradamus to Buridan and Don Juan — the largest body of Zévaco's work ever rendered into English.

With The Gaboriau Project, still in its infancy, he has begun the same work for Émile Gaboriau, the inventor of the detective novel — new translations for an author English readers have largely known through nineteenth-century versions, starting with The Lerouge Case. His original fiction pursues a parallel programme, updating the great French works for the present day: Hugo's Les Misérables carried to America, Voltaire's Candide to the Donbass. He has also translated the anarchist memoirs of Alexandre Marius Jacob, the real-life inspiration for Arsène Lupin.

Michel Zévaco · 1860–1918

Dumas's Great Rival, in English at Last

Every novel Michel Zévaco published — translated one by one, until the canon is complete

Michel Zévaco was the great rival of Alexandre Dumas in the hearts of French readers: an anarchist journalist turned master of the swashbuckler, whose cloak-and-dagger serials gripped Belle Époque France. Jean-Paul Sartre hailed him as a writer of genius; his novels have been read by millions in Persian, Turkish, Arabic, Romanian and Spanish. Yet for over a century, they remained almost entirely unavailable in English.

The Zévaco Project exists to end that silence. Its goal is simple and unprecedented: every novel Michel Zévaco published, translated into English, one by one, until the canon is complete. No publisher, in more than a hundred years, has attempted it. For readers of Dumas, Sabatini, Féval and Pérez-Reverte — swordfights, secret passages, doomed loves, poisoners, jesters and kings, in English at last.

A Second Canon

The Gaboriau Project

Émile Gaboriau (1832–1873) invented the detective novel: his Monsieur Lecoq was deducing two decades before Sherlock Holmes existed. Yet English readers have largely known him through nineteenth-century translations. The Gaboriau Project — young still, and growing — gives the father of the genre a modern English voice, novel by novel, starting where the genre itself started.
